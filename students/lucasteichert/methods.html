Methods

Methodology

Study Design

Our group first watched Twelve Nights to learn more about shelter dogs and what problems they have. We also went to Ruifang Animal Shelter two times. This helped us see the real situation, not only from the movie. We also talked to people at the shelter. We are not sure if they were workers or volunteers, but they helped us understand more about the dogs and the shelter. After that, we talked about what topic we wanted to work on. We used the fishbone diagram and 5 whys to help us find our action point. In the end, we chose the topic of bringing dogs closer to people. We chose this because we thought if more people can meet dogs in a safe way, more people may notice them and maybe adopt them. Our research question was what is the best way to help dogs get closer to people?

Participants

>For our survey, we asked 50 people in a park next to Daan Junior High School. Four people were Daan students, and the other 46 people were strangers we asked in the park. We did not ask for gender because it was not important for our topic. We mainly used age groups to organize the participants. The age groups were 0-20, 21-40, 41-60, and 61-75. One person did not say their age.

Materials/Tools


Our group used a poster, printed question sheets, talking notes, paper, and pens. The printed question sheet had the full question list. The poster showed Q2 and Q3, so people could read those questions and choices more clearly. The talking notes helped the speaker know what to say when introducing the survey. We used paper and pens to write down the answers. Our survey had five questions about dogs, adoption, problems people may have, transportation, and ways to help dogs get closer to people.

Procedure

First, we made our group with Jasper, Lucas, Justin, and Alfie. Then we gave everyone a job. Alfie was the speaker and asked the questions. Justin was the recorder and wrote down the answers on paper. Jasper was the helper and helped show the poster and question sheet. Lucas was helper 2 and helped support the group. We did the survey face to face in a park next to Daan Junior High School. Before asking the questions, we explained that our project was about bringing dogs closer to people and learning what people think about dog adoption. After that, we asked for the person's age, and then we asked Q1 to Q5. The questions asked if they had been to a place where they could meet dogs, what difficulties they might have, what ways could help dogs get noticed, what transportation would be easiest, and what they thought was the best way to help dogs get closer to people. After we got 50 answers, we organized the answers into tables. For the questions with choices, we counted the answers. For the open-ended questions, we put similar answers into groups.

Special Considerations

When we did the survey, we tried to be polite and clear. We explained the survey first so people knew what they were answering. We also tried not to ask more than one person at the same time, because that could make the answers messy. We used the poster for Q2 and Q3, and the printed question sheet for the full survey. One thing our group did was give everyone a clear job. Alfie talked to the participants, Justin wrote down the answers, Jasper helped show the poster and question sheet, and Lucas helped support the group. This made the survey easier to do. Since we were asking strangers in a public park, we tried to keep the survey simple. We only asked for age because we needed it for grouping the data. We did not ask for extra personal information.

Data Analysis

The answers are organized into tables so they are easier to read. For the questions with choices, each answer choice is counted. The numbers are also changed into percentages so the answers can be compared. For the open-ended questions, similar answers are put into the same groups. This is because people may use different words but have a similar idea. The answers are also compared by age groups. The age groups are 0-20, 21-40, 41-60, and 61-75. Tables and charts are used to show the data more clearly. This helps people see the patterns in the answers. This section only explains how the data is organized and compared. It does not report the final results of the survey.
