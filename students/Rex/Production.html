Intro

This semester, we have a class talk on shelters and stray dogs, and a project to film a video about adopting dogs. My teammates are Neo and Sonny. Our idea is to pretend that a dog is an applicant and interview for the position called “The best friend”. We changed the term for what dogs do to a professional term. We think this is an interesting idea.

Script chinese ver

《面試：最忠誠的朋友》
預計片長：4 分 00 秒
拍攝視角：全程主觀鏡頭（POV＝面試官視角）
語言：繁體中文
核心情緒：幽默 → 溫暖 → 留白共鳴

【0:00–0:25】場景一：面試室｜日｜內
（畫面為面試官主觀視角。手持微呼吸感，眼平高度。）
（一張木桌佔畫面下緣。桌面右側放著一張A4職缺公告，標題清晰：「應徵職位：最忠誠的朋友」。左側有一支鋼筆、一杯冷水、幾份空白履歷。）
（鏡頭微調，模擬面試官調整坐姿。椅腳輕微摩擦聲。）
（敲門聲。兩聲，不疾不徐。）

面試官（POV畫外音）：請進。

（門推開。自然光切進半張臉。求職者走入：舊夾克、磨損帆布背包、頭髮微亂但眼神清亮。步伐平穩。）
（他走向空椅。坐下前，停頓半秒，低頭輕嗅椅墊邊緣，才端正落座。）
（手指無意識在膝蓋上輕敲，節奏穩定如拍子。）

【0:25–1:45】場景二：面試室｜日｜內
（鏡頭維持中近景。環境音極簡：紙張翻動、筆尖輕叩桌面、空調低頻聲。）

面試官（POV）：先自我介紹。你認為自己適合這個職位嗎？

求職者（坐姿端正，語氣如背履歷）：您好。我對「長期陪伴」有實務經驗。專長包括：情緒穩定支援、24小時居家警備、精準環境偵測，以及……準時叫您起床。

（筆尖在紙上劃過兩下。鏡頭微前傾，表示面試官靠向前。）

面試官：履歷上寫你擅長團隊合作？

求職者：非常擅長。只要給點正面回饋，我能快速建立信任。我很會傾聽，通常不打斷對方，除非……您提到「散步」兩個字。

面試官（輕笑，紙張翻頁聲）：你的缺點是？

求職者（停頓兩秒，認真思考）：看到拋擲物會短暫性方向感失靈。還有，我不太習慣馬桶。但我保證，絕不在沙發上畫地圖。我受過基礎訓練。

面試官：畫地圖？

求職者（迅速正色）：抽象藝術。還在進修。

（鏡頭微拉遠半寸。求職者手指敲擊節奏加快，偶爾歪頭，視線緊盯鏡頭中心。）

【1:45–2:55】場景三：面試室｜日｜內

（光線自然轉暖。空調聲淡出。僅留呼吸與衣料摩擦聲。）

面試官（語氣轉平穩）：薪資福利方面，你期望什麼？

求職者（語速放慢，眼神真摯）：不用年終獎金。只要一個乾燥的角落、一碗乾淨的水、週末能陪您走走。如果偶爾有人拍拍我的頭……我會覺得這輩子值了。

（安靜 2 秒。遠處隱約傳來街道車流聲，隨即消散。）

面試官：你目前沒有固定住址？

求職者：公園長椅、騎樓、橋下都待過。但我的行李很輕，只有一顆準備好愛人的心。

面試官（椅腳輕移，語氣轉柔）：你知道嗎？這個職位不簽勞基法。

求職者（微愣）：那簽什麼？

面試官（站起聲，鏡頭隨之微抬）：簽「家人」。任期一輩子。福利包括：無限次摸頭、沙發共享權，以及……永遠被需要的感覺。

（求職者眼睛亮起。右手迅速伸向鏡頭前方，掌心向上。）
（鏡頭微降，模擬面試官伸手握手。兩手交握入畫 1 秒。）

求職者：我接受。但先聲明，如果您的薯條掉在地上，基於資源回收原則，我必須代為保管。

面試官（笑出聲）：成交。

【2:55–3:40】場景四：面試室｜日｜內
（收筆聲、合文件夾聲。求職者鬆肩，嘴角帶笑。）

面試官：明天帶行李來報到。別遲到。

求職者（立刻站直，點頭）：保證準時。不會遲到，只會提早。

（他轉身走向門口。步伐輕快，背包肩帶隨動作輕晃。門開，光線灑入側臉。）
（他停步，回頭望向鏡頭方向。手指在褲縫輕敲兩下，節奏漸緩。）
（門輕輕關上。房間安靜。桌上職缺公告被微風掀起一角。）
（鏡頭緩緩推近公告。字體清晰：「最忠誠的朋友」。）

【3:40–4:00】場景五：黑畫面｜靜音
（畫面瞬間切黑。所有環境音、呼吸聲、衣物摩擦聲完全停止。靜止 3 秒。）
（螢幕中央緩緩浮現白色繁體字，字體簡潔無襯線，字距略寬：）
狗狗也需要家
（字幕停留 5 秒後淡出。全片結束。）

Script English ver

The Interview: Most Loyal Friend
Estimated Runtime: 4 minutes 00 seconds Perspective: Full POV (Interviewer’s point of view) Language:
English Core Emotion: Humor → Warmth → Resonant Silence

【0:00–0:25】 Scene 1: Interview Room | Day | Interior
(The screen is the interviewer’s POV. Handheld camera with a slight "breathing" sensation, at eye level.) (A wooden table occupies the lower edge of the frame. On the right side of the table lies an A4 job posting with a clear title: "Position: Most Loyal Friend." On the left is a fountain pen, a glass of cold water, and several blank resumes.) (The camera adjusts slightly, simulating the interviewer shifting in their seat. The faint sound of chair legs rubbing against the floor.) (A knock at the door. Two knocks, steady and unhurried.)

Interviewer (POV Voiceover): Come in.

(The door opens. Natural light cuts across half of the face of the applicant. He enters: an old jacket, a worn canvas backpack, hair a bit messy but eyes bright and clear. He walks with a steady pace.) (He approaches the empty chair. Before sitting down, he pauses for half a second, leans down to sniff the edge of the seat cushion, and then sits down properly.) (His fingers unconsciously tap on his knees with a steady, rhythmic beat.)

【0:25–1:45】 Scene 2: Interview Room | Day | Interior
(The camera maintains a medium close-up. Minimal ambient sound: the rustle of paper, the tip of a pen tapping the desk, the low hum of the air conditioner.)

Interviewer (POV): Start with a self-introduction. Why do you think you are suited for this position?

Applicant (Sitting upright, speaking as if reciting a resume): Hello. I have practical experience in "long-term companionship." My specialties include: emotional stability support, 24-hour home security, precision environment detection, and... waking you up on time.

(The pen scribbles twice on the paper. The camera leans forward slightly, indicating the interviewer is leaning in.)

Interviewer: Your resume says you’re good at teamwork?

Applicant: Very much so. As long as I’m given positive feedback, I can build trust quickly. I’m a great listener; I usually don't interrupt, unless... you mention the word "walk."

Interviewer (A soft chuckle, sound of a page turning): And your weaknesses?

Applicant (Pauses for two seconds, thinking seriously): I suffer from temporary loss of direction when I see a thrown object. Also, I’m not very used to toilets. But I promise, I will never "draw maps" on the sofa. I’ve had basic training.

Interviewer: Drawing maps?

Applicant (Quickly turns serious): Abstract art. I’m still practicing.

(The camera pulls back half an inch. The rhythmic tapping of the applicant’s fingers speeds up; he tilts his head occasionally, his gaze fixed on the center of the lens.)

【1:45–2:55】 Scene 3: Interview Room | Day | Interior
(The lighting naturally turns warmer. The AC hum fades out. Only the sound of breathing and fabric friction remains.)

Interviewer (Tone becoming steadier): Regarding compensation and benefits, what are your expectations?

Applicant (Slowing his speech, eyes sincere): I don’t need an annual bonus. Just a dry corner, a bowl of clean water, and someone to walk with on the weekends. If someone could pat my head every now and then... I’d feel like this life was worth it.

(Silence for 2 seconds. The faint sound of street traffic drifts in from the distance, then dissipates.)

Interviewer: You don’t have a fixed address at the moment?

Applicant: I’ve stayed on park benches, under verandas, and beneath bridges. But my luggage is light; I only carry a heart ready to love.

Interviewer (Sound of the chair moving slightly, tone turning soft): You know, this position doesn't follow standard labor laws.

Applicant (Slightly stunned): Then what do we sign?

Interviewer (Sound of standing up, the camera rises slightly): We sign for "Family." The term is for a lifetime. Benefits include: unlimited head scratches, sofa-sharing rights, and... the feeling of always being needed.

(The applicant’s eyes light up. His right hand quickly reaches toward the camera, palm up.) (The camera lowers slightly, simulating the interviewer reaching out to shake hands. The two hands meet in the frame for 1 second.)

Applicant: I accept. But full disclosure—if your fries fall on the floor, based on the principles of resource recycling, I must take custody of them.

Interviewer (Laughs out loud): Deal.

【2:55–3:40】 Scene 4: Interview Room | Day | Interior
(The sound of a pen being capped and a folder closing. The applicant relaxes his shoulders, a smile on his face.)

Interviewer: Bring your bags and report for duty tomorrow. Don't be late.

Applicant (Immediately stands straight, nodding): I promise. I won't be late; I'll only be early.

(He turns to walk toward the door. His pace is light, the backpack straps swaying with his movement. The door opens, light spilling onto his profile.) (He stops, looks back toward the camera. His fingers tap twice against the seam of his trousers, the rhythm slowing down.) (The door gently closes. The room is quiet. A corner of the job posting on the desk is lifted by a slight breeze.) (The camera slowly zooms in on the posting. The text is clear: "Most Loyal Friend.")

【3:40–4:00】 Scene 5: Black Screen | Silent
(The screen cuts to black instantly. All ambient sounds—breathing, movement, fabric—stop completely. Stillness for 3 seconds.) (In the center of the screen, white text slowly fades in. The font is clean, sans-serif, with wide spacing:)

Dogs need a home, too.

(The subtitles stay for 5 seconds before fading out. Film ends.)

Reflaction During filming, I faced some challenges. First, the audio was missing. I took the rough cut audio to replace it, but because the length between those two is different, I’ll need to work on it more. The second challenge I’m facing is that I need to act like a homeless dog. It is kinda hard for me cause it’s embarrassing for me to do something like stick out my tongue. But finally, I still do it, just trying to convince myself it’s ok.

I’ve been learning that planning is really important, and our team sometimes didn’t bring our props, and delay the time. Next time should be clearer about what we should do now, and not just follow the teacher’s deadline.
